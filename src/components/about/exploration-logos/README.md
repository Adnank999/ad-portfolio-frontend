# Exploration shader logos

The three learning cards use real beveled/extruded geometry, not SVG artwork: a nested shader prism, a hollow modeling cube, and a backend/AI chip. The reference video's saturated metallic color comes from reflecting a colored environment, not from matte paint. The shared `src/components/home/shaders-glsl/assets/colorA.jpg` therefore drives the reflection environment as well as a subtle object-space surface tint. High metalness and moderate-low roughness retain the moving pink, purple, red, and amber reflections. Low clearcoat, limited white specular intensity, and lower exposure restrain glare without washing the finish into pastel colors. Pointer and keyboard focus gently influence the artwork; existing card text, disclosures, and layout remain unchanged.

`geometry.ts` builds the meshes. `material.ts` exposes metalness, roughness, clearcoat, and reflection intensity. `shaders.ts` projects a light tint across all faces without replacing Three.js's physical lighting. `environment.ts` surrounds the meshes with the color image and four restrained colored panels, then prefilters that studio once with PMREM; the same reflection target serves all three logos. Subdued pink/purple direct lights replace white hotspots, and no bright white softboxes bleach the reflected colors. Slow full turns and a gently rotating environment make color follow the viewing angle as in the reference. A fitted perspective camera and rounder six-segment bevels preserve the dimensional appearance; hover changes remain subtle.

`renderer.ts` uses one transparent WebGL2 canvas with three scissored views, rather than opening a context per card. It bounds pixel ratio and caps drawing at 60 FPS (30 on compact viewports). The renderer and its shared image are loaded near the cards, pause offscreen and in hidden tabs, and dispose geometries, materials, the image texture, PMREM target, listeners, observers, and context when leaving About. Reflection prefiltering waits for the image to load. The CSS fallback stays visible until the colored environment and the first real frame are ready; image failures do not reveal a black canvas.

The pause control freezes all three logos, including their colors and environment rotations. Reduced motion renders a still WebGL frame rather than running a loop. CSS-only geometric fallbacks are visible during loading, without JavaScript/WebGL, and after context loss; restoration regenerates the reflection target and redraws the artwork. No per-frame reflection capture, postprocessing passes, or new dependencies are required. `data-logo-state`, `data-logo-activity`, and `data-logo-frames` expose renderer state for browser checks.
